# Question Bank by SundarSTEM — Project Guide

This is the plain-language guide to how the whole portal works — written for Saffi, teachers, and admins, not for an AI or a developer (that's what `CLAUDE.md` is for). If you want to know "what does this button do" or "why does the app behave this way," this is the file to read.

---

## What this is

Sundar STEM School runs the **Pakistan Math Contest (PMC)** — a talent-identification exam for ages 10–13, split into four categories:

| Contest | Age | Curriculum anchor |
|---|---|---|
| PMC-4 | 10 | Beast Academy Book 3 |
| PMC-5 | 11 | Beast Academy Book 4 |
| PMC-6 / PMC-7 | 12 / 13 | Beast Academy Book 5 |

This portal is where a team of teachers collaboratively **write, tag, peer-review, lock, assemble into papers, and upload** every question that ends up in a live PMC exam. It lives at `https://saffiullahmalik.github.io/pmc_qb/`, runs entirely in the browser, and is free to operate (Firebase's free "Spark" plan) — there's no server to maintain.

## Who can do what

Every person who can sign in has one of seven roles, set by an admin:

- **Teacher** — can write, edit, and comment on any question; can rate and like questions; can be assigned chapters or peer reviews.
- **QB Lead** — everything a teacher can, plus can lock a question (once peer review agrees with it) and unlock one back to "In Review."
- **Admin** — everything above, plus: create/remove teacher accounts, delete questions, manage workload assignments, set daily limits, add curriculum, and see the full activity log.
- **External Reviewer** — a separate, read-and-comment-only account for an outside spot-checker. They see the normal Question Bank tab — same filters, same search, every question regardless of status — so they can browse and pick whatever they want to check, exactly like everyone else; they just don't get a "+ New Question" button, and can't edit, delete, or change a question's status once they open one, including already-locked ones. Their comments carry a purple "External Reviewer" tag so they stand out in the thread, and if they comment on a locked question, the admin viewing it sees an explicit prompt to unlock it and apply the fix.
- **Uploader** — can only see the Upload tab, nothing else. Within it they can do everything a teacher could there: browse a paper by category/day/slot, download each question's image, and mark it uploaded (or undo). They can't mark a whole paper's upload work complete — that stays admin-only. Every question now shows its correct answer letter right there (and a collapsed "Answer key" grid for the whole paper at a glance) so they can set the right option in Quilgo without needing Bank access — once they've done that, a second **"Mark answer set"** button (next to "Mark uploaded") tracks it as its own step, separate from "the image is up." Downloaded images are also named after the paper now — `"<contest name> Day<N> Slot<N> Q<NN>.png"` — so a folder full of downloads sorts itself into the right upload order instead of every file being named after the question's own title.
- **Automation** — not a person; a role for a service account like the one behind the hourly Drive backup (see SETUP.md). Same read/write access as a Teacher, but left out of the Team/By-teacher charts and the Assignment board's columns so it never shows up as a confusing empty "teacher" next to your real staff.
- **Student Reviewer** — reviews student exam submissions for integrity only, nothing else — no Bank, no question-making tabs at all, just the Review Students tab. Pick this role when someone's whole job is checking students for integrity breaches, with no authoring duties. If instead you want a teacher who ALSO does student-integrity review on top of their normal work, don't change their role — just tick the "Student reviewer" checkbox next to their name in the Teacher accounts table (Admin → Manage teachers) instead; that keeps every tab their actual role already gives them, and adds Review Students on top.

There's no public sign-up — an admin creates every login from **Admin → Manage teachers**.

## The life of a question

1. **Draft** — a teacher writes it: English + Urdu prompt, a figure or a shared equation if needed, 2–8 options, difficulty, chapter, skill(s) tested, and a required solution (with an optional image attached to it too, e.g. a worked diagram — separate from the question's own figure, and never shown to students, only wherever the solution itself is shown: peer review, the drawer, and the printed Solution Manual).
2. **In Review** — the moment a question is moved here, the portal automatically assigns it to a *different* teacher as a reviewer — see "Peer review" below. The author can keep editing while it's in this state; other teachers can comment at any point regardless of status.
3. **Peer review happens** — the assigned reviewer solves the question independently first (without seeing the marked answer), then the screen reveals the marked answer and the author's solution so they can compare it against their own. Only then do they say how it went:
   - **✅ OK — reviewed, ready to lock** → the question becomes lockable.
   - **⚠️ Ambiguous wording / ❌ Wrong answer marked / 🔀 Multiple solutions possible / ✏️ Other issue** → a comment explaining which of these it was (plus any note) is posted automatically, and the question **cannot be locked** until the author fixes it and a re-review says OK. Saving an edit after being sent back automatically sends it to the *same* reviewer again — it shows up in their queue tagged **"Re-review"** so it's obvious this is a second look, not a new assignment. (This is also why a reviewer's "Reviewed" count can go down, not just up: once a question they flagged gets resaved by its author, it drops out of their completed count until they look at it again — nothing was lost, it's just waiting on them a second time.)
   - Writing up your own working/solution and logging your time are both optional — only picking an answer and one of these five outcomes is required.
4. **Locked** — a QB Lead (or admin) locks it once review has agreed. A locked question's content can't be edited by a QB Lead anymore — only an admin can, or a QB Lead can unlock it (set it back to "In Review") first. This step still works exactly as before and is still the only thing that actually freezes a question's content — but it's no longer required before step 5.
5. **Used in a contest** — an admin assembles questions into a paper (Contests tab). A question is eligible once it's **Locked**, **Uploaded**, or just **Reviewed OK** (peer review agreed, even before a QB Lead has locked it) — tagged with its category/day/slot, the question itself then shows a "Used in: [paper name]" tag. Picking a "Reviewed OK" question this way does **not** lock it — its author can still edit it afterward, unlike a genuinely locked one, so lock a question first if you need its content frozen before it goes into a paper.
6. **Uploaded** — each question is downloaded as an image and uploaded to the exam delivery tool one by one, tracked per question (in which contest/day/slot) so nothing gets uploaded twice or missed.
7. **Uploaded** (status) — after a test cycle, admins can also record post-test statistics (p-value, discrimination index) against the question to flag ones worth revising for next time.

## Daily limits (quality control)

An admin sets four numbers (**Admin → Daily limits**):

- **Daily creation target** (default 15) — hit this many days in a row and you build a streak.
- **Daily creation cap** (default 20) — the hard stop; you can't create a 21st question today no matter what. The 15–20 range is "bonus" work beyond the daily target.
- **Daily review cap** (default 20) — the same idea, for how many peer reviews you can submit in a day.
- **Review backlog block** (default 50) — "review first, write second": once a teacher has more than this many peer reviews waiting on them, they can't start a brand-new question until they clear some (editing an existing draft still always works). An admin can also pause a specific person's question-making directly, for any reason, from the "Paused" checkbox next to their name in Teacher accounts — that works regardless of their backlog.

These exist so a rush to hit a quota never comes at the cost of quality — the cap is a ceiling, not a target.

## Difficulty balance (per assigned chapter)

Each assigned chapter's target splits evenly across the three difficulties — a 15-question target means 5 Medium, 5 Hard, 5 Very Hard (any target not divisible by 3 rounds up on Medium and Hard first). The editor shows where you stand against that split live as you pick a chapter and difficulty, and **won't let you save** a question in a difficulty that's already full for that chapter — pick a different difficulty instead. This only applies to chapters actually assigned to you; self-initiated questions outside an assignment aren't balance-checked.

## Peer review, in detail

- **Assignment is random but balanced, with a fair-share cap** — when a question goes "In Review," the portal looks at every other teacher/QB Lead *who has an assigned chapter* and picks randomly among whoever has the fewest reviews ever assigned to them in total, so far. On top of that, nobody is asked to review more than the team's average authoring load — if 4 teachers are each assigned 180 questions to write (720 total ÷ 4), nobody gets asked to review more than 180 total either; past that, they simply stop getting picked until everyone else catches up. Admins, External Reviewers, Uploaders, and anyone with no assignment at all are never picked. Your own "Reviews" card on the Bank tab shows a pie of assigned-vs-done, right next to your "Your questions" pie, plus your own number against that cap.
- **Admin → Admin & Analytics → Review workload** — one card per reviewer, with both of those pies (authored, and reviews assigned/done) and their current total against the fair-share cap, sorted most-loaded first. If someone's stuck with an old backlog, type how many to move and hit **Shift to others** — it moves their oldest pending reviews to whoever currently has the least load, spread across more than one person if needed rather than dumping them all on one other teacher.
- **The reviewer sees who made it and what it's for** — before solving anything, the review screen shows the question's level, type, difficulty, skills, author, and when it was posted/last updated, plus its chapter and subtopic, so you know the context before you dig in.
- **The reviewer solves it blind, first** — the review screen hides which option is marked correct. They pick their own answer; writing up their working and logging their time are both optional.
- **Then the marked answer and solution are revealed**, side by side with what the reviewer answered, and only then do they make the call: OK and ready to lock, or flagged with a specific reason (ambiguous, wrong answer, multiple solutions possible, or something else) that sends it back to the author. That's a real, specific signal — not just "disagree" — and it's exactly what this step exists to catch before a question reaches a live contest.
- **Every review posts a comment now — OK included, not just a flag** — with whatever working/solution and time the reviewer entered carried along automatically, so the author actually sees what the reviewer did, not just the final verdict. These comments are colored and badged ("Peer review: OK" in green, "Peer review: Flagged" in red) so they stand out from a regular comment, everywhere comments show up — the question's View screen, its card in the main list, and now the editor too (read-only there — reply from View). Because comments never get overwritten the way the review verdict itself does on a resubmit, the full story stays visible in order: flagged, then fixed, then OK — not just whatever the status says right now.
- Your "to review" queue shows up on the Question Bank tab's dashboard (click **My reviews** to see the full list and pick any of them, or go into one and use ← Previous / Next → to move through them in either direction — you're never stuck doing them in a fixed order) and the Activity feed.
- Separately, an **External Reviewer** account (see "Who can do what") can browse and spot-check any question at any time — including already-locked ones — independent of this balanced rotation.

## Gamification

A thin strip at the top of every tab shows your current streak, today's progress toward the daily target, this week's total, and how many reviews you still owe. The Question Bank tab's right-hand panel goes further:

- **Sent back — needs your fix** — only appears when a reviewer has actually sent one of your questions back, listed with the specific reason (ambiguous, wrong answer, etc.) right at the top of the panel, above everything else — click one to jump straight into fixing it. A flagged question also gets a small red "⚠ {reason}" tag on its card in the main list, so it's visible to anyone scanning the Question Bank tab too, not just on your own dashboard.
- **Your assignments** — one thin bar per PMC level showing how much of your assigned quota is locked (with a ✓ once a level hits 100%); click a level to expand it into its individual chapters, each showing its own draft/in-review/reviewed breakdown underneath.
- **Your questions** — a donut of your own draft/in-review/reviewed/locked breakdown. "Reviewed" means a peer already agreed with the answer — it's just waiting on a QB Lead to lock it, which is different from still being actively reviewed.
- **Last 7 days** — a bar per day of how many questions you created.
- **Team** — every active teacher as a small cluster of five separate bars labeled by initials (e.g. "Imdad Hussain" → "IH"), sorted by total output (creating *and* reviewing both count), with your own cluster bolded so you can find yourself at a glance — hover any bar for the full name and exact count. The five bars are draft (red), in review (brown), reviewed (green), locked (blue), and reviews done (violet, i.e. how many of *other people's* questions you've reviewed) — all five sit on the same height scale across every teacher, so you can directly compare, say, how many each person has locked just by eye, not by guessing at segment lengths inside one bar.
- **Reviews** — a **My reviews** button opens a list of everything assigned to you, split into "To review" and "Reviewed" tabs, so you can see and pick from everything in your queue instead of only ever being handed one at a time; a donut below shows how often your completed reviews came back OK vs. sent back. Inside an actual review, ← Previous / Next → buttons let you move back and forth through everything assigned to you.

## Every tab, in one line each

- **Question Bank** — the full list of questions, with filters (including author, "made before/after a date", and a status filter that separately breaks out "Reviewed — ready to lock" from the rest of "In Review" so you can see exactly how many are waiting on a lock versus still being worked), sort, and your personal dashboard at the top. Each card shows its comments and rating inline (collapsed past 5 comments, with a "+N more" link) so you can see the conversation without opening it. The editor's last step lets you pick a font and text alignment for English and Urdu separately (in addition to the existing size/line-height controls), on top of the inline bold/italic/underline/color formatting — defaults stay exactly as before, these are opt-in. Clicking a question now opens a centered pop-up sized to show the whole question properly (same for a delete confirmation or any other small pop-up) instead of a narrow panel sliding in from the side. A card's status tag now correctly says "Reviewed OK" once a reviewer has agreed, instead of still saying "In Review" — matches what you filtered for, instead of looking like the filter didn't work.
- **Activity** — the 30 most recently posted questions across the whole team, so everyone knows what's new and needs a comment.
- **Contests** — assemble contest-ready questions (locked, uploaded, or Reviewed OK) into a paper (tagged by category/day/slot), print a bilingual exam + answer key + solution manual. Each question in the picker shows a status pill so you can tell Locked from Reviewed OK at a glance. Picking questions for a new paper now has real filters — search text, chapter, difficulty, skill, author, a sort order, and a "Hide already-used" toggle — plus "Select all filtered" and "Clear selection," and every question shows its full text instead of a truncated snippet, so picking from a large bank is actually manageable.
  - **⚡ Build session automatically** — builds a whole round at once instead of picking every paper by hand. Pick a PMC category, how many days, how many slots per day, questions per paper (30 is the default — one full round), and whether questions can repeat across this round's own papers or not. Before you commit, it shows exactly how many locked/reviewed-OK questions exist right now per chapter and difficulty against what the round needs, so a shortfall is visible up front. "Build now" randomly fills every paper from eligible questions; if there aren't enough yet, it still builds everything it can and shows the session as **Pending** with the exact chapters/difficulties still short — come back and click **Fill remaining slots** once more questions are locked, and it only fills in the gaps, never touches what's already been placed. The real 3-day/2-slot/30-question round follows the exact chapter/difficulty table you specified; any other combination of days/slots/question-count uses an automatically balanced distribution instead. A question used in one round can still be picked again in a *later* round — only reuse *within the same round* is what the overlap setting controls — and every single placement is recorded on that question's own "Used in" tags (hover one to see which round, day, and slot), so there's always a full history of exactly where a question has been used.
- **Upload** — pick a paper by category → day → slot, download each question as an image, mark it uploaded, with a record of who uploaded what and when.
- **Assignment** — a drag-and-drop board for admins to hand out chapters (or custom tracks like IQ/Logic reasoning — if none exists yet, a link right there points to where to add one) to teachers, plus a dashboard of who's assigned what and a team comparison chart.
- **Curriculum Map** — the full Beast Academy chapter reference, plus any custom books/chapters an admin has added (this is also how a cross-cutting track like "IQ & Logic Reasoning" gets added — it doesn't have to belong to just one PMC level). Any teacher can add a new subtopic to a chapter right from the question editor (a "+ New" button next to the Subtopic field). From Admin → Curriculum, an admin can edit or remove anything added this way, add chapters/books, and — via the "Edit any chapter" picker — edit the **title and topics of any chapter at all, including the official Beast Academy ones** (e.g. B3-C12). A chapter's code (like `B3-C1`) always stays fixed, since every question tagged to it relies on that code never changing.
- **SOP** — the official school procedure document, for reference inside the app. An admin can edit it directly (✎ Edit button) — heading and body text per section, plus one font size and line height for the whole page; a blank line makes a new paragraph, and a line starting with "- " becomes a bullet point.
- **Admin & Analytics** — teacher accounts, daily limits, curriculum additions, backups, post-test statistics, and the activity log (admin-only). The longer lists (Pending reviews, Review workload, Curriculum entries, Scored questions, Activity log) are collapsed behind a "▸ Click to expand" header by default so the page doesn't turn into one long scroll — click any of them to open it; Teacher accounts stays open since it's checked often. Teacher accounts also has a **Student reviewer** checkbox per person, separate from their role — anyone can be a student reviewer on top of their normal job — plus a **Paused** checkbox that blocks that person from starting a new question until you untick it, regardless of their review backlog.
- **Post Processing** (admin-only, new) — where a completed exam's results get turned into real analysis. **Import roster**: pick the PMC type/day/slot, upload a roster CSV (Form B, Name, District, School, Father Name), and it's saved against that exact sitting. **Import results**: pick the same paper, upload the actual Quilgo results CSV for it — it's matched to the roster by Form B, there's a preview showing how many rows and question-columns it found (with an override table if the column order ever needs correcting by hand), and "Import results" writes one record per student. A second button, **Run post-processing**, then computes each question's p-value/discrimination/attempt count from those real student answers and saves it — the same place "Scored questions" in Admin & Analytics already reads from, so that table now shows real numbers instead of needing them typed in by hand. **Item Analysis**: tick one or more sittings (same as Student Performance below) to see each question's p-value/discrimination, alongside its chapter/skill tags — tick several sittings a question was reused across (e.g. the same item used in more than one slot) and its stats combine automatically. Below the table: an average-discrimination-by-skill chart, an average-p-value-by-difficulty chart, and a skill × difficulty grid so you can spot which combination of topic and difficulty is producing weak questions. **Student Performance**: tick one or more sittings that have had results imported (tick several to combine, e.g. all 4 PMC-4 slots, or PMC-4 + PMC-5 for a combined merit list), hit Load, and you get an integrity table first — only students who trip one of the flag thresholds (tab/window switches, face match/presence %, trust score, blank camera shots, multiple screens, suspicious photos), with those thresholds editable right there and applied instantly — then a score-distribution chart, a "top performers — question breakdown" table (which questions even your strongest students are missing), and a full ranked merit list with a CSV export. Flagging a student asks for a reason, excludes them from the ranked merit list (greyed out, not deleted — Clear brings them back), and keeps a permanent record of why. Tick students in that integrity table and use **Assign checked to reviewers** to split them across everyone marked as a student reviewer — "Randomly" balances load one student at a time; "By district"/"By school" keeps a whole city or school together with one reviewer.
- **Review Students** (visible if you're the Student Reviewer role, or a teacher ticked as a reviewer on top of their normal role) — shows the students an admin assigned to you from the Student Performance tab, with the same integrity signals and Flag/Clear action admins use. Flagging asks for a reason and excludes that student from the merit list; Clear reverses it. If your account's role is set to Student Reviewer, this is the only tab you'll see.

## Admin controls, summarized

- **Who's assigned what** → Assignment tab's Kanban board (drag a chapter into a teacher's column, or use the "+ Add a chapter" dropdown; remove with the ✕ on a card).
- **How much can be created/reviewed per day** → Admin → Daily limits.
- **Who has access at all** → Admin → Manage teachers (create, promote/demote, remove) — including setting up an External Reviewer account. Click the ✎ next to anyone's name to fix it if it's showing as their email instead (happens if "Full name" was left blank when the account was created) — it updates everywhere that name is shown (Assignment board, Team chart, reassign menus) right away.
- **A full audit trail** → Admin → Activity Log (who did what, when — admin-only).
- **A safety net** → Admin → Export all data as JSON, openable with `backup-viewer.html` if the live site or Firestore is ever unreachable — just double-click it and load the backup file. Its **Questions** tab has the same filters as the live Question Bank tab (level, type, difficulty, status, chapter, skill, author, date range, sort, search), and every question — draft or unreviewed included, not just locked ones — can be previewed and downloaded as an exam-ready image, so there's always something usable for a paper even mid-outage. Its **Upload status** tab specifically shows every contest's questions still pending upload (and which are already done, by whom and when), same **Download image…** button. That button needs internet access to load the same rendering libraries the live app uses; everything else in this viewer works with no network at all. It can't mark anything as uploaded itself (it can't write anywhere) — do that in the live app once it's back.
- **Automated hourly backups** → `backupFirestoreToDrive.gs` (a Google Apps Script, set up once via script.google.com — full instructions are a comment at the top of the file) saves a fresh backup JSON to a Drive folder every hour on its own, so there's never more than about an hour of data to lose track of if something goes wrong. Grab the newest file from that Drive folder any time and open it with `backup-viewer.html`.
